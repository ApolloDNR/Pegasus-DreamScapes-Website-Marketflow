# User Test Script — pegasusdreamscapes.com

Per Master Blueprint v5.1 §32.16: test three critical end-to-end
journeys with representative people (not designers or developers)
before final visual lock. 2–3 participants is enough; 20–30 minutes
each. Run on the participant's own phone for at least one session.

## Setup (per session)

Give the participant nothing but the URL. Do not explain what Pegasus
is. Ask them to think out loud. You observe and take notes; never help
unless they are fully stuck (and note where that happened).

Open with two questions before they click anything, looking only at the
homepage first screen:

1. "In your own words, what does this company do?"
2. "Who do you think this site is for?"

## Journey 1 — Owner (property owner with a complicated situation)

Framing to read aloud: "Imagine you inherited a house in the East Bay
that needs work, and you're not sure whether to sell it, fix it, or
something else. Use this site to figure out what you'd do next."

Expected path: Home → Property Owners → intake → confirmation.

Watch for:

- Do they find Property Owners from the nav or the homepage router
  ("What are you bringing to Pegasus?")?
- On /property-owners, do they recognize their situation in the list?
- Do they understand "Bring an Opportunity" means "contact/submit"?
- In the intake, does the first step ("What are you bringing to
  Pegasus?") make sense? Do they pick "A property I own"?
- Do they hesitate at any form field? Which one?
- After submitting: do they know what happens next, and in what
  timeframe? (The page says a person reads it; response within 48
  hours; no agency created.)

## Journey 2 — Deal source (wholesaler / deal finder)

Framing: "Imagine you found an off-market house and have it under
contract, but you need a buyer or a partner to close it. What would you
do on this site?"

Expected path: Home → Deal Partners → intake (deal path) →
confirmation.

Watch for:

- Do they find Deal Partners?
- Does the "Every deal is missing something" / composer framing make
  sense to a deal person?
- Do they notice the "On the record" note (source attribution recorded;
  compensation agreed in writing before distribution)? Does it read as
  protection or as a warning?
- In the intake, do they pick "A property under contract" or "A lead or
  opportunity" correctly for their situation?
- Do they trust that Pegasus won't go around them? What gives or takes
  away that trust?

## Journey 3 — GP / operating partnership

Framing: "Imagine you run deals yourself but you need an operating
partner in the East Bay — construction, local execution, or capital
support. What would you do here?"

Expected path: Home → Deal Partners → operating-partnership lane →
inquiry.

Watch for:

- Do they distinguish the partnership lane from the deal-submission
  lane?
- Does "The role is chosen, not assumed" on /how-we-operate land, if
  they visit it?
- Do they find the partnership CTA and understand what happens after?

## Additional checks (any session, time permitting)

- **Strategy Lab Quick Read:** from the homepage, ask them to "get a
  quick read on a property." Do they find Strategy Lab? Does the
  welcome step ("Start with a single property.") make the tool's
  purpose clear? Do they understand the result is preliminary and not
  advice?
- **Work With Apollo:** ask "if you just wanted a realtor to sell your
  normal house, what would you do here?" Do they find the licensed
  lane? Is the Pegasus-vs-KW relationship clear or confusing?
- **Mobile navigation:** on a phone — open the menu, reach Our Work,
  return home, tap Bring an Opportunity. Any mis-taps or dead ends?
- **Error recovery:** in the intake, try to continue past a required
  field left blank. Is the error understandable? Can they recover
  without starting over?
- **Returning to a saved intake:** if they abandon the form and come
  back, does their progress/expectation match what happens?

## Record for every participant (Blueprint v5.1 §32.16)

1. What they believe Pegasus is (their words, verbatim).
2. What they think Pegasus will do for them.
3. Where they hesitated (exact page + element).
4. Whether they trust the company — and the moment trust moved either
   direction.
5. Whether they found the correct next action unaided.
6. Whether any regulated relationship appeared confusing — especially
   the Pegasus Corp. vs. Keller Williams licensed-representation
   distinction, and whether Peggy read clearly as an AI assistant.

## Pass / act thresholds

- All three journeys completed unaided → visual lock proceeds.
- Any participant misreads Pegasus as a brokerage, or reads Peggy as a
  human → copy fix before launch (compliance-sensitive).
- Two participants hesitate at the same element → redesign that element
  before launch; one participant → log it for post-launch iteration.
